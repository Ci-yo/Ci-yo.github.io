R–01 · 2025Research Group LeaderStructural bioinformatics

Protein Surface Representation and Interaction Prediction Based on MaSIF

Can protein interfaces be represented in a way that captures geometry and chemistry well enough to model interaction and affinity—and what does that representation actually learn?

Approach

Implemented a MaSIF-inspired surface convolutional workflow in PyTorch, trained and evaluated interaction tasks, and compared surface representations with graph-based alternatives.

My contribution

Led the research group work, implemented the surface model, handled data splitting and training, designed PPI binding-site and affinity tasks, and analysed model outputs against comparable methods.

Public evidence

The curated public showcase contains 937 retained MaSIF-style prediction rows with MAE 0.926, RMSE 1.308, and Pearson r 0.815, plus an independent evaluator and reproducibility notes.

Research direction

The project pushed my interests from static prediction toward molecular perturbations, matched comparisons, and mechanistic interpretation of variants and PTMs.

R–02 · 2026Research Group Memberm6A omics

Bioinformatics Benchmarking of an IVT Calibration System for m6A Omics Data

How robust is an integrated m6A signal when the evidence comes from technologies with different biases, error modes, and coverage?

Approach

Benchmarked a hierarchical Bayesian m6A integration model across 2.79 million candidate sites and 14 technologies using leave-one-technology-out evaluation against statistical baselines.

My contribution

Developed Random Forest analyses distinguishing true and false m6A calls, investigated technology-specific detection errors, and built reproducible cross-technology comparison workflows.

Orthogonal evaluation

Evaluated robustness with IVT evidence and writer/eraser perturbation datasets rather than relying on one internal benchmark alone.

What this added

It made calibration, distribution shift, and fair comparison permanent parts of how I think about biological machine learning.

R–03 · 2024Research Group MemberEnvironmental data

Patterns and Drivers of Methane Emissions from Rivers and Streams Under Climate Change

Which geographic, environmental, and hydrological variables travel with changes in river methane observations—and how cautiously can those associations be interpreted?

Approach

Collected, cleaned, and analysed GRiMeDB records with R; used statistical analysis and modelling to identify variables associated with methane emissions and compare future climate scenarios.

My contribution

Worked from heterogeneous records to analysis-ready data, statistical relationships, and model interpretation while keeping the distinction between association and causation explicit.

Dataset scale

The underlying database records referenced in the project span 5,029 sites, 24,024 methane concentration measurements, and 8,205 methane flux measurements.

Public layer

The curated repository exposes a smaller 1,113-row matched analysis subset, scripts, model tables, provenance notes, and a reproducible atlas.
